www.Daycare is a project that investigates the phenomena of children's content made on YouTube. A research project initiated by the eerie feeling that occurs when watching the seemingly innocent, colorful and upbeat nursery rhymes.

What does the content look like? Sound like? How is it compared to traditional programming?

Why and how is this made, and available for free?

And how does this way of producing media affect the viewers?

The project is an installation that resembles a lab environment, in which participants are invited to watch a video playing on an Ipad, that emmulates the diffrent kinds of stimuli that occurs in childrens programming, rangeing from traditional childrens TV programming (typically low-stimuli), to youtube kids nursery rhymes (typically high stimuli). Using pixilations and audio blurring, participants are able to experience the videos removed from its context.

The system of content production is controlled by attention. What is paid attention to, and for how long. The longer children keep attention, the more money. The less attention put on google and the content creators, the less reliable they are.

▸

Cocomelon is number 5 most subscribed channel on YouTube. Their main/only content consists of animated nursery rhymes aimed at toddlers. One nursery rhyme will reappear in many diffrent video compilations on their channel. Each video will usually have over 100 million view - and as per YouTube policy creators get paid pr. 1000 views. Nothing in cocomelons content in terms of themes, lyrics etc are directly alarming. Cocomelon gets paid more the longer the watch time for each video is. Therefore the production of these videos are a direct commodification of children's attention.

Good Morning everyone, and welcome to laboratory 4-57. Today is the first unveiling of research project 00-00-01 – or colloquially named www.Daycare.

The aim of this project is to address the phenomenon of children’s content uploaded to youtube, how it differs from traditional TV, as well as the effects this may have.Today you will be attending a lab trial. I have two test subjects, who will be subjected to a video that has been constructed by my team, which is me. The aim of the video is to represent the different kinds of stimuli that children are exposed to, when consuming digital video content. The video that you are hearing and seeing on the monitor is the same that our test subjects are viewing on the handheld screens. initiating trial in 3…2…1….

ok, now, you can press play *press play*

The intro video is playing – I take notes.

the clip that just played is a simulation of high stimuli content that has been algorithmically enforced. Here I am referring to auditory and visual digital stimuli.I urge you to focus on what you are seeing and hearing, this is not about content or storyline. My team, me, have surveyed a variety of sources. Specifically from Contemporary public broadcast TV from the following stations:

danish broadcast service

The bbc

And popular youtube channels such as:

Meow Meow Kitty

To construct this simulation we have focused on cocomelon, the by far biggest youtube channel with 151 million subscribers and over 148 billion views. With 500 h of content being uploaded to youtube every minute it has been crucial to the quality of research to focus on the content with the biggest impact.

pause and take notes

I urge you to try to focus on everything you are seeing and hearing, this is not about content and storylines. there are no ABCs, nursery rhymes or moral lessons. Can you spot what content has been algorithmically optimized?

waves at test subjects - no reaction

So, when looking a the video, how much are you seeing and hearing? are you able to concentrate on my words, are you feeling your attention is somewhere else? that’s ok! Just know that this content is effectivicced to stimulate dopamine production and dopamine is a highly addictive drug. excess in dopamine can cause irritability, difficulty concentrating, short term memory loss, anxiety and a general dissatisfaction with the pace of life.

worried look. taking notes

I urge you to focus on what you are seeing and hearing, this is not about content or storyline. The clip that is playing is a simulation.Some has been algorithmically enforced. YouTube’s algorithm to be exact. Based on youtubes recording of every childs interactions with the platform, every click, and watchtime is recorded. It’s a profit game. how many clicks, and how long can we keep the attention, for the cheapest content possible. children love repetitive and easy to digest content, they are highly susceptible clickbait and easy to entertain.

On screen you have been seeing “pixel tests .” they have been derived from different kind of children’s content and pixelated in order to remove content, and purely focus on amount of movement, clip lengths and color saturations. The clips have been taken from content both from youtube – mainly cocomelon – as well as a variety of traditional TV programming. This has been accompanied by the sound layer that has been engineered to emulate different sound intensities that appear in the different kinds of content.

this research is not aiming to shame caretakers for using digital tools to make everyday life easier, but rather start a discussion on what content is healthy to consume, beyond themes and storylines. What does it look and sound like, and what are the intensities? Its a discussion relevant for content aimed at all age groups – but especially urgent for age groups with developing brains.

This is the base for a body of research needed to better understand the vast world of content uploaded online. The world of online content is so rapidly developing that the creation and consumption of the content is based on instincts, what feels good is what feeds algorithms. But what are good tools for our everyday life, how can we implement guidelines or moral codes. All of this requires more knowledge and understanding of a virtual space that still remains very new and unexplored. An understanding is what will help us move away from the feeling that the digital is “not real” or only “in the clouds”, but realize that it is very much part of our real world, and has the power to permanently alter how we perceive it. And lastly it is an urgency to do these considerations for the sake of the generations coming after us.

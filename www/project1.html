Green Green Grass of Home is an exhibition piece that centers the audience as part of the landscape of Google street view. In Front of a green screen, and with use of a joystick the user can navigate and explore the city. The exhibition is aimed to explore how the human body interacts with digital spaces. Here, emerging themselves in a digital replica of their home, they interact, by following prompts given on screen.

Google street view in its popularity gets to shape how the world is represented and by extension understood. Who, what, when and how the city is represented is dictated by a faceless super cooperation in silicon valley. The representations feel unfamiliar, and uncanny. With the body as a tool, the uncannyness of the emersion reveals the shortcomings in googles depiction. The people faceless, places frozen in time, purely visual and spacial, and without emotion. Google street views is revealed as a contenuiation of colonial cartography practices, and a direct product of the free and open internet.

video essay - uncanny neigbourhoods

here the research around google street view was explored in a video format. The video explores how Google street view uses photogrammetry to flatten your local neighbourhood. By using my own scans and distorting the meshes, the flatness is revealed. Relating this flatness to role that Google plays in shaping our understanding of the world, and the colonial practices of cartography that they are engaging in. For this movie, I placed myself in the uncanny neighbourhood scans, using green screen, playing with movement and scale to expose the awkwardness.
